# Expansion proposal: <client>

One paragraph, in this order: the evidence from the report (with its numbers and dates); the seat it points to; what that agent does and never does; its one number and how you'll measure it; how long the pilot runs inside the retainer; where the price comes from.

Sam's, for Harbor & Home (fiction): The pilot report named two weekend voicemails that waited until Monday, because the quote desk never sees voicemail. By day 90 there were seven (two in the pilot month, five in the two months after it), and every one was returned on Monday morning. The next seat is phone messages after hours, the second seat in the chapter 10 offer, in its smallest form: the agent reads the transcript of each weekend voicemail, which Dana's phone service emails to the hello@ inbox, and drafts a reply and a call-back note for Dana to send or delete. It never calls anyone, never speaks, and never names a day, a time or a price the facts file doesn't state. A voice that answers the phone waits for Dana's voice and likeness policy. The one number is the median hours from a weekend voicemail to the first reply a person sent, counted from the inbox and Dana's call log. It runs thirty days inside the retainer, with a decision at the fourth Friday review, and its price is a line on the retainer from pricing.md, agreed after those thirty days.

# Maintenance triggers (any one of these means: update, rerun the cases, note it in the runbook)
- The client changes an offer, a price, a service area or a rule the agent quotes.
- A route changes: a new inbox, a new form, a new phone line.
- The tool changes how it behaves, or a model changes underneath it.
- The person who approves changes (a new office manager, a new owner).
- A file the agent needs goes missing, or two files disagree.
- A promise on the client's site no longer matches the facts.
