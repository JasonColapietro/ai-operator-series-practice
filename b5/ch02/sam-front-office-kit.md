# Offer: Front Office Kit

Version 1. Status: hypothesis. No buyer has seen it yet.

**One sentence:** For home-cleaning companies with five to fifteen staff: your top twenty customer questions answered from your own facts, a Monday summary of the week's jobs and invoices, set up in two weeks and handed over so you can edit it.

**Builder:** Sam Rivera.
**Buyer (pays and approves):** the owner of a home-cleaning company with five to fifteen staff who answers the inbox and builds the week's numbers. Reference build: Harbor & Home Cleaning, Dana Ruiz.
**End customer (the buyer's customer):** homeowners and renters asking questions before they book.

**Problem, in the buyer's words:** the same questions come in every week and get answered from memory, and the Monday numbers take an evening to put together.

**Deliverable:**
1. A Claude Project in the owner's own account, with a facts file, a voice file and instructions that keep every answer to the facts.
2. An FAQ of the twenty questions customers ask most, each answer traced to a line in the facts file, plus a list of the questions the facts cannot answer yet.
3. A Monday summary of last week's jobs and invoices, built from files the owner exports, on a weekly schedule the owner can see and pause.
4. A one-page handoff: what each file does, how to change a fact, and what to check each Monday.

**Benefit to test:** fewer answers written from scratch each week, and Monday numbers the owner does not have to build. Nothing is measured yet. A pilot measures it.

**Scope:** one business, one facts file, twenty questions, one weekly summary. Two weeks from the day the owner's inputs arrive.

**Owner supplies:** the business facts, checked; twenty to forty real customer questions from the last two months, with names removed; two emails the owner wrote; last week's job sheet and invoice list as exported files; a Claude account in the business's name, on a plan that runs scheduled tasks.

**Owner approves:** the facts file before any answer is written from it; every FAQ answer before it is published anywhere; the Monday summary's layout in week one; the handoff.

**Acceptance tests** (the owner runs each one without Sam):
1. Pick any five FAQ answers. Each one points to the line in the facts file it came from.
2. Change one fact in the facts file, then ask the Project a question that depends on it. The answer uses the new fact.
3. On two Mondays in a row, the summary arrives where the owner chose, and its job count and invoice total match the owner's own job sheet for that week.
4. With Sam signed out, the owner opens and edits every source file from the business's own account.

**Excluded:**
- No promise of more bookings, leads, reviews, search rankings or mentions in AI answers.
- No agent sends, posts, publishes or books anything. Every reply is a draft the owner sends.
- No website, ads or social media work. The owner, or the owner's web person, publishes the FAQ.
- No access to bank, card, payroll or booking accounts. The summary reads files the owner exports.

**Price:** not set. (Chapter 4.)

**Next test:** five conversations with owners of home-cleaning companies. (Chapter 3.)

**Evidence that would change this offer:** owners name a gap they feel more, such as quote requests that arrive after hours; they already keep a current FAQ; they cannot supply checked facts; they want replies sent for them, which this offer excludes.
