HARBOR & HOME CLEANING: VOICE SAMPLE
How Dana writes, on one page, then the five emails it was drawn from. Use them to match tone. Every fact comes from the business facts file, never from these emails.
Updated: November 13, 2026

PERSPECTIVE
- I write as the owner: "I" for me, "we" for the team.
- They asked a question, so the answer comes first and the explanation second.
- A plain no beats a promise we can't keep. When we get something wrong, I say so in the first sentence.

STRUCTURE
- A yes: the answer, the one detail they need to act on it, then one next step they can answer in a line.
- A no: one plain sentence, then what we can do instead, if there's something. Never another company.
- A complaint: own it, then the fix, then thanks for telling me.
- Four to eight sentences, in paragraphs of two to four. No headings or bullet points in an email.

VOCABULARY
- "Hi" and their name: a first name if they used one, Mr. or Mrs. if they didn't.
- Phrases I use: "Thanks for getting in touch." "That's on us." "Let me know which works."
- Contractions everywhere (we're, I'll, that's). Short plain verbs: come back, fix, finish, send.
- Prices are "from" prices, with the square footage they cover and what they include.
- Sign off "Dana". On a first contact, add the business name and phone number from the facts file.

THEMES
- What a price or a service includes.
- Fixing a miss quickly, at no charge.
- The crew, by first name, when the customer has met them.
- What the customer wants in their own home.

NEVER
- No exclamation points.
- No "Dear valued customer", "reach out", "don't hesitate to" or "I hope this email finds you well".
- No "thrilled", "delighted", "takes great pride" or "the Harbor & Home family".
- One "That's on us" is the whole apology. No apology paragraph.
- No day, time or price that isn't in the facts file or my request, and no promise about which crew will come unless I say who.
