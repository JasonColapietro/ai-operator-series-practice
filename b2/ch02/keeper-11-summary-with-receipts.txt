## Keeper #11: Summary with section receipts
Saved: [TODAY'S DATE]
Use it for: a long document someone has to decide about

Job: I uploaded [DOCUMENT]. Summarize it in about one page of plain English for [READER], who has to decide [DECISION].

Material: Use only the uploaded document. End every point with the section it comes from, in brackets, like this: [Section 4.2]. If you can't tie a point to a section, it stays out of the summary.

Shape: Six parts, in this order.
1. Overview in five lines: who, what, how long, how much, and how it ends.
2. Key dates and deadlines, as a table: Date or deadline | What happens | Section.
3. Money, as a table: every fee, charge and credit, with its amount.
4. Two short lists: what we have to do, and what [OTHER PARTY] has to do.
5. Things to watch: terms that could cost us money or take us by surprise.
6. Problems in the document: sections that contradict each other, and references to a section or attachment that isn't there. Quote the exact words, and don't decide which one wins.
When you use a legal term, explain it in a few plain words.

Check: Under each row of the Key dates table, quote the sentence from the document that supports it, word for word. Where the document is silent on something a reader would expect, such as a start date or a price, write "Not stated." No legal advice. Where a point needs a lawyer's opinion, flag it in one line.
