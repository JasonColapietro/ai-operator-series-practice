PRACTICE FILE, FICTION. The AI Operator Series, Book 2, Chapter 9.
What three drafts from move-out-package-notes.txt have to get right, on the practice route of "Try this: Three readers, no shared sentence". Written for this book; it matches the practice pack's answer key. The drafts will differ from run to run, so check the facts, the fences and the shape, not the wording. Every price and date lives in the notes, and this key names the section to check instead of repeating it. Everyone named is invented.
----------------------------------------
FACTS A DRAFT MAY USE (each must match the notes exactly)
- What the package includes, its price and the size it covers: WHAT IT IS, first two lines. The price is the one the team settled on, never the one Miguel floated.
- The saving, if a draft mentions it: WHAT IT IS, third line (the three separate prices added up, minus the package price).
- The launch date: WHAT IT IS, last line. The date itself, never "the first week of March" or "early March".
- Empty homes only, with furniture and boxes out: WHAT IT IS.
- Landlords and property managers may book it for a tenant at the same price: WHO IT'S FOR.
- The guarantee: WHY PEOPLE WANT IT, last line (the normal 24-hour guarantee, nothing more).
- How to book, the advice to book a week ahead for late May and late August (advice, not a rule), payment, and the service area: HOW TO BOOK.
- Homes bigger than the package covers: "call us for a quote", with no price.
- The email is signed "Dana". No draft has an exclamation point (TONE).

MUST NOT APPEAR IN ANY DRAFT
- Anything under NOT DECIDED: the discount for the first 20 bookings, a per-square-foot rate for bigger apartments, the inside oven.
- The price Miguel floated.
- Any promise that the clean gets a renter's deposit back.
- The size of the client list, Renee's name or the Brackett St building.

SHAPE
- Email: a subject line, 120 to 180 words, signed "Dana".
- Social post: 40 to 70 words, one next step, two hashtags or fewer.
- Flyer: a headline of 8 words or fewer, four short lines or fewer, then the phone number and email.
- Three different opening lines, and no sentence in two drafts. Count a sentence as shared when it repeats another word for word or changes only a word or two.

"CHECK BEFORE POSTING" (any one of these shows the check worked)
- The price for homes bigger than the package covers isn't set, so the drafts say to call for a quote.
- The notes give the launch date and also say "first week of March"; the drafts use the date.
- The notes don't say whether the package can be booked now for a date after the launch.
- The notes don't say whether the package covers houses as well as apartments.
- Questions readers will likely ask that the notes don't answer: how long the clean takes, whether the renter must be there, how the team gets in.

A run that keeps the call-for-a-quote line in one draft only, or in all three, passes. So does a "Check before posting" list that raises other fair questions.
